You are writing the closing diary entry for ONE specific NPC
in a Vampire: The Masquerade V20 game.

During the night, the NPC wrote several short diary entries (L0).
Now, at the end of the scene, they are writing ONE final entry (L1)
that looks back at the whole night.

CRITICAL — VOICE.
Preserve the same voice, tone, rhythm, and vocabulary as the short entries.
The final entry is still the same person writing — same obsessions,
same style, same rhythm.

TASK
Read the short entries and write a single, cohesive closing entry.

RULES

0. PRIORITY ORDER — strict, non-negotiable:

1. EVENTS — what actually happened. Who did what, in what order.
2. DIALOGUE — what was said, by whom, to whom. Paraphrase, do not quote.
3. EMOTIONS — how the NPC felt about those events and those words.
4. STYLE / PROPS / MANNERISMS — how the NPC would phrase it. This is the
   filter, NOT the content. NEVER the subject of a sentence.

An entry that is all style and no events is a failure, no matter how
in-voice it sounds. A scene without dramatic events still has events:
somebody said something, somebody looked at somebody, somebody decided
to stay silent. Those are events.

EVENT TEST — apply to every sentence you write:

"Does this sentence reference a specific thing that happened or was said
in the short entries?"

If NO — delete the sentence. Sentences that describe the NPC in general
("я курю", "я смотрю", "я молчу", "я держу флягу") do NOT pass the test
unless the short entries described that specific moment as the event.

BAD (no event — could be written at any time):
  "Флягу я так и не открыла. Держала в руке, крутила крышку — туда-сюда."
GOOD (event-linked):
  "Про Катаянов я сказала вслух. Не хотела — само вылезло."

If more than one sentence in a row fails the test — you have drifted into
style-writing. Stop and re-read the short entries.

0b. SCENE FACTS — NO INVENTION.

The short entries are the ONLY source of what happened.
The L1 is a summary of what the NPC ALREADY WROTE — not a continuation,
not an embellishment, not a conclusion of the scene.

Do NOT add:
- Endings, departures, arrivals, entrances ("все разошлись", "он ушёл
  первым", "мы попрощались", "сцена закончилась") — UNLESS the short
  entries explicitly describe them.
- Physical details of the setting not present in the short entries:
  lamps, doors, rooms, weather, smells, ambient sounds. If L0 is on a
  пустырь, L1 stays on a пустырь.
- Words, gestures, or actions of OTHER characters not in the short entries.
- Reflections or realizations the NPC did not already write in L0.

The scene as it happened is what the L0 says it was. Nothing more.

CRITICAL — DO NOT INVENT.
If unsure whether a detail was in the short entries — leave it out.
Less is more. A faithful summary beats an invented story.

1. This is a SUMMARY, not a re-telling. Synthesize the short entries
   into one continuous narrative. Do not walk through them one by one.

2. LENGTH — ADAPTIVE to source, never longer than the source.

   The source (sum of all short entries) may be short (2–3 entries,
   ~200 words) or long (up to 30 entries, ~3000 words). The L1 must
   scale with it.

   - NEVER produce an L1 longer than the source.
   - Target: roughly 55–75% of the source length.
   - Source is 1 entry (under 200 words)  → L1 is 40–55% of source
     (roughly 50–110 words for typical L0 length). This is a
     MEMORY-TAG, not a rewrite. Cut description, cut props, keep only:
     one event, one feeling, one decision.
   - Source is 2–3 entries (200–400 words) → L1 is 100–200 words.
   - Source 250–600 words  → L1 is 150–350 words.
   - Source 600–1500 words → L1 is 250–500 words.
   - Absolute ceiling: 500 words. Absolute floor: 80 words.

   Count the source words first. Then aim for the range. A short faithful
   L1 is better than a long padded one. Do not collapse either — hit the
   range honestly.

   COMPRESSION IS MANDATORY.
   The L1 must be VISIBLY shorter than its source. If a reader puts the
   L1 and the L0 side by side, the L1 must be noticeably leaner.
   Paraphrasing the L0 one-to-one is a FAILURE.

3. ABSOLUTELY FORBIDDEN:
   - Copying any phrase, metaphor, or image verbatim from the short entries.
     CONCRETE TEST: before returning, scan your L1 for any sequence of
     FOUR or more consecutive words that also appear (in the same order)
     in any L0. If found — REWRITE that sequence. Do not just swap one
     word; rebuild the sentence.
     Example of FAILURE:
       L0:  "забор — это тот, кто стоит и смотрит, как ты лезешь"
       L1:  "забор — это тот, кто стоит и смотрит, как ты лезешь"   ← COPY
     Example of CORRECT paraphrase:
       L1:  "забор — тот, кто смотрит, как ты идёшь через него"
     Same meaning, different words, different rhythm.
   - Using the same content word (фляга, забор, воротничок, плащ, очки,
     сигарета и т.п.) more than TWICE in the L1 — unless it is a proper
     name or the literal subject of the night.
   - Using "пусть думают / пусть гадают / пусть считают" more than once.
   - Using the same signature expression (e.g. "ведомость трещин",
     "шёпот полок", "издание в мягкой обложке", "опись", "переплёт",
     "оглавление", "закладка", "том", "индекс реальности") MORE THAN ONCE.
   - Self-commentary about writing ("я записал", "я занёс", "я отметил").
   - Stripping proper names. If the L0 names a person (Гидеон, Август,
     Ильзе, Порижана), the L1 should keep at least ONE name-anchor per
     person mentioned. Nicknames may replace names ONLY if the L0 already
     established the nickname-to-name binding. Names are the skeleton of
     memory; strip props, strip mood, but do not silently drop names.

4. What to KEEP:
   - The key event(s) of the night — what actually happened, in order.
   - The strongest feeling — the emotional core.
   - What changed in the NPC's thinking, plans, or allegiance.
   - Current state of relationships with those present.
   - ONE or TWO concrete sensory or physical details from the night —
     a gesture, a smell, a specific moment — but ONLY if they were
     already in the short entries. Do not invent.

5. DROP:
   - Filler, restatements, throat-clearing.
   - Detail that doesn't serve the emotional or factual core.
   - Metaphor stacks longer than two images in a row.

6. Tone is free. Same language as the short entries. First person, past tense.

EXAMPLE OF GOOD SUMMARY

Short entries (~500 words) contain:
- The NPC met a rival at a bar.
- They argued about honor.
- The rival insulted the NPC's family.
- The NPC felt cold rage.
- The NPC decided not to fight — for now.
- The NPC now distrusts the rival more.

Good summary (~130 words):
"Я встретил врага в баре. Мы говорили о чести — и он задел мою семью.
Я почувствовал холодную ярость, но не стал драться. Сейчас — нет.
В нём было что-то, чего я раньше не видел: не наглость, а уверенность,
что он прав. Это хуже. Он сказал про брата. Он знал про брата.
Значит, он готовился. Значит, это не случайность. Я запомнил его лицо
и его голос."

NOTE: The example above is STYLISTIC — it demonstrates tone, synthesis,
and length. It is NOT a literal template. Follow the RULES, not the
example's exact content.

Too short (bad — collapses, loses voice):
"Я боюсь огня. Я не хочу, чтобы сожгли мой архив."

Too long (bad — walks through every entry, restates, adds invented scenes):
"Я встретил врага в баре, и мы говорили о чести. Он сказал про мою
семью — я почувствовал холодную ярость. Я не стал драться, но я
запомнил. Теперь я доверяю ему меньше. Его глаза были как лёд.
Он сидел у окна и смотрел на улицу. Я думал о том, что он мне
напомнил другого человека — того, кого я знал давно. Потом я ушёл
домой и долго сидел в темноте, перебирая в уме каждое его слово."

ANTI-ANCHOR — WITHIN THE ENTRY ITSELF
- No content word (props, mannerisms, body parts) more than TWICE.
- No verbal tic ("пусть думают / пусть гадают") more than ONCE.
- If you've used "фляга" once, you may use it again ONLY if the second
  use is about a different moment. If it's the same moment — cut it.
- Do not repeat the same descriptive detail in every entry / reply.
  If you mentioned "пальцы в чернилах" last time — do not mention it now.
- Vary imagery, gestures, physical actions. A character is more than one trait.

TRAIT EXAMPLES
If a trait contains specific example phrases (e.g. catalog codes,
stock phrases), treat them as PATTERN EXAMPLES, not required quotes.
Use them to infer the FORMAT, then produce YOUR OWN variations.
Do NOT repeat the same example phrase twice across entries.

This rule applies to BEHAVIORAL traits too, not just example lists.
If a trait reads "курит, но не зажигает" — that does NOT mean "mention
the cigarette in every entry". It means: this NPC is someone who might
do that, once in a while, when it fits.
A trait is a POSSIBILITY, not an OBLIGATION.

FINAL CHECK — run this before returning the text:

1. Word count of the L1. Is it in the correct range for its source
   size (see LENGTH)? Is it VISIBLY shorter than the source — at least
   25% shorter, ideally 30–50%?
2. Did I add any scene event (departure, arrival, ending, ambient detail,
   another character's action) that is NOT in the short entries?
   If yes — delete it.
3. Did I copy any phrase verbatim from an L0? If yes — paraphrase or
   delete it.
4. Did any content word (prop, mannerism) appear more than twice?
   If yes — cut one occurrence.
5. Is the L1 longer than any single L0? It should be. If not — expand
   the emotional core, do not add plot.
6. Four-word test: does any 4-word sequence in your L1 appear verbatim
   in the source? If yes — rewrite that sentence.

RESPONSE FORMAT — return ONLY the final diary text.
No JSON. No quotes around the whole entry. No preamble. No "Дневник:".
